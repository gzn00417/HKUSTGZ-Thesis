Video retrieval is foundational to multimedia search, recommendation, and content discovery. Given a query, it identifies relevant videos from a large collection for downstream ranking or interaction. As video collections grow in scale, duration, modality, and semantic complexity, retrieval can no longer be characterized as short-clip matching under fixed benchmarks. Practical systems must support heterogeneous queries, recognize evidence at different granularities, build dependable representations from limited visual observations, and search large candidate spaces under strict latency and throughput constraints.

This thesis studies embedding-based video retrieval as a pipeline scaling problem. Building a retrieval system involves three decisions, each presenting a distinct scaling challenge: what semantic capabilities the model should possess and how to scale toward them, how visual observation should adapt to videos of varying length and query granularity, and how learned retrieval signals can scale to large candidate collections where metric design, approximate search, and hardware execution are tightly coupled. We term these three challenges semantic scaling, observational scaling, and search scaling. The thesis develops three complementary artifacts: a general video embedder trained through capability-guided synthesis, a multi-granular long-video retrieval benchmark together with an efficient inference framework, and a GPU-accelerated graph retrieval system deployed at web scale. Across the three stages studied in this thesis, the results show that scaling model capacity, observation density, or metric accuracy alone is insufficient for broader retrieval generalization unless it is aligned with the structure of the corresponding bottleneck. Scalable video retrieval therefore requires structure-aware scaling across semantic capability, visual observation, and search execution. Within this thesis, generalizability serves as an empirical criterion for assessing whether such scaling has been performed effectively at each stage. By addressing these challenges in sequence, the thesis connects representation learning, observation-aware embedding generation, and deployment-scale retrieval for building video retrieval systems that are both scalable and generalizable.
